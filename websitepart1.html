Chinese cuisine

Chinese cuisine comprises cuisines originating from China, that varies with the country's large population and geographic diversity[1], as well as from Chinese people from other parts of the world. Due to the Chinese diaspora and the historical power of the country, Chinese cuisine has profoundly influenced other cuisines in Asia and beyond, with modifications made to cater to local palates. Chinese food staples like rice, soy sauce, noodles, tea, chili oil, and tofu, and utensils such as chopsticks and the wok, can now be found worldwide.

Pizza

Pizza is traditionally made with a yeast-leavened dough that forms the base, which is typically round but can also be rectangular or square. The most common base toppings include tomato sauce and cheese, especially mozzarella. Additional toppings can vary widely and include vegetables, meats, seafood, and herbs, allowing for countless combinations and styles.

Pizza is typically cooked in an oven, traditionally a wood-fired pizza oven for a distinctive flavor and texture. It can be served fresh and hot, by the slice, or as whole pies in restaurants or takeaways. Pizza has become a staple of casual dining and fast food culture worldwide, enjoyed for its versatility and ease of customization. In summary, pizza is a versatile dish defined by its dough base, sauce, cheese, and a variety of toppings, with deep cultural roots in Italy and widespread popularity globally.
